Dawn came to August City the way it always did after a bad night: gray, indifferent, and too early. Kael sat on the curb outside the café, the script page folded into his jacket pocket and the rift core heavy in his palm. He turned it over again. The faint pulse under the facets answered his thumb like a second heartbeat.

He heard footsteps and didn't look up. Mira dropped onto the curb beside him, a paper cup of coffee in each hand. She passed one to him.

"You look terrible," she said.

"Thanks."

"Did you sleep at all?"

"No." He took a sip. It was hot enough to hurt. Good. "The Gate closed. The stage manager is dead—or deleted, or whatever. And my Panel still shows that line."

Mira pulled up her own Panel with a flick of her fingers. Kael watched the translucent blue surface hang in the air between them. Level 2. No extra slots. No weird words. She swiped through her skills and quests as if checking for invisible ink.

"Mine just says the Gate is closed. No cue. No next act." She let the Panel dissipate. "The Warden mentioned the stage manager wasn't scheduled. So who put it there?"

"That's the question." Kael pocketed the rift core and pulled out the script page. The ink hadn't faded with daylight. The line at the bottom still read: VERSION 1.0: ACT II CUE — NEXT GATE IS A DOOR MADE OF SIGNS.

"Signs," Mira repeated. "Like street signs?"

"Or guild signs. Shop signs. Warnings." Kael stood up. "The rift before was in a theater. The one before that was a sewer mouth. The System has been using the city as a stage. So if it says the next Gate is a door made of signs, maybe we should go looking where signs are made."

"A print shop?"

"Or the central station. They rebuild the departures board there every day. Hundreds of signs. Times, platforms, destinations."

Mira gave him a long look. "You want to go back to the station."

"The Gate showed up there once already."

"And you almost got flattened by a version announcement."

"I got flattened. Then I got back up." Kael touched his side where the stage manager's last hit had landed. The bruise was still purple under his jacket. "We need to know what Act II means. The System said version 1.0 was the end of the tutorial. If we don't find that door, the next rift might find us."

Mira finished her coffee in three long gulps and crushed the cup. "Okay. But we go in through the north entrance, past the ticket office, and we stay away from the main concourse until we know what we're dealing with."

"North entrance it is."

They walked. August City's morning traffic was just beginning to grumble along the main roads. Delivery vans idled outside bakeries. A woman with a bright yellow umbrella argued with a street vendor about the price of chestnuts. None of them looked at Kael and Mira. Panels had been part of life for months now, and two teenagers walking with purpose wasn't worth a glance.

Central Station loomed by the time the sun cleared the rooftops. It was a hunk of pre-Panel architecture: stone pillars, a great arched glass roof, and a clock tower that had stopped at 11:59 three years ago and never been repaired. The north entrance was smaller, tucked between a luggage shop and a shuttered bank. Its sign was plain white with black letters: AUGUST CENTRAL STATION — NORTH.

Kael stopped in front of it.

"What?" Mira asked.

"The sign." He pulled out the script page and held it beside the sign. "The System wrote: NEXT GATE IS A DOOR MADE OF SIGNS. North entrance. White sign. Black letters. It's a door. With a sign on it. But that can't be all."

"Maybe it's not the front door." Mira walked past him, into the station's lower hall. Kael followed.

Inside, the air smelled like stale coffee, floor wax, and the particular ozone tang of Panels being toggled open and closed. Commuters moved along the tiled floor in rivers. The departures board loomed above the central staircase, a colossal grid of orange letters and numbers that flipped and clattered with every announcement.

Kael stared at it.

Board. Sign. Hundreds of signs, all changing at once.

"There," he said, pointing at the board's southwest edge. A block of text had frozen there, letters no longer flipping: GATE 14 — RIFT SEASON — NEXT DEPARTURE: UNKNOWN.

The other passengers didn't seem to see it. Mira's eyes widened. "That wasn't there yesterday."

"The System's been editing the station again." Kael moved toward the staircase. "Gate 14. That's the old platform, the one they closed after the tracks buckled. No one uses it."

They pushed through the crowd, up the stairs, and along a corridor lined with faded posters advertising routes to towns that no longer existed outside August City. Platform 14 sat at the end of the corridor, separated by a rusted gate. A sign on the gate, cheap and laminated, read: DO NOT ENTER.

"Door made of signs," Mira murmured.

"Or gate made of signs." Kael stepped closer. The gate was old steel, but the sign on it was bright and clean. He reached out with his free hand. The moment his fingers touched the laminated corner, the script page in his jacket grew warm.

He pulled it out. New ink was spreading across the bottom in thin, spidery letters: ACT II SCENE 1 — THE PLATFORM IS A STAGE. THE TRAIN IS A PROMPT. BOARD THE LAST CAR. DO NOT BRING THE CORE.

Kael read it aloud. Mira went still.

"Don't bring the core?" she said. "The rift core? The thing we've been carrying since the sewer?"

"It says the train is a prompt. Maybe the core's a different prompt. If I board with it, I might trigger the wrong scene." He shifted his weight. "I'll leave it with you. You stay on the platform."

"Kael—"

"I'm not dying. But I'm also not walking into Act II with a glowing rock that makes stage managers come unscheduled. I need to know what the System wants me to see."

Mira's jaw tightened, but she held out her hand. Kael gave her the rift core. It pulsed once, resentfully, then settled into her palm.

"Don't be a hero," she said.

"I'm never a hero. I'm just the guy with the extra slot." He turned and pushed the rusted gate open. The laminated sign peeled and curled, but the gate swung wide.

Platform 14 stretched before him, empty and dark. The glass roof overhead was cracked, and morning light fell in weak shafts through the grime. Where the tracks should have been, there was a single train: sleek, black, and impossibly clean, with gold letters along its side spelling AUGUST LINE — ACT II.

Its doors were open.

Kael walked toward the last car. The train waited, patient as a held breath. He stepped inside. The doors closed behind him with a soft hiss. No conductor. No seats. Just a long aisle with a single paper ticket lying on the floor.

He picked it up. Printed on it, in the same spidery ink: SIT WHERE THE LIGHT DOESN'T TOUCH. WATCH FOR THE MAN WHO KNOWS YOUR NAME.

Kael looked up. At the far end of the car, a figure sat in the shadow between two dim emergency lights. The man wore a worn gray overcoat and a cap pulled low. His face was obscured, but his voice was clear, dry, and familiar.

"Kael of August City," the man said. "You're early. I appreciate that. Punctuality is a forgotten virtue."

"Who are you?" Kael took a step forward. The script page in his hand trembled.

"I'm the reason the stage manager wasn't scheduled. I'm the reason your Slot Zero doesn't belong in any build I know. And I'm the one who left the cue for this door." The man tilted his head. "Sit down. We have a scene to rehearse."

Kael didn't move. "Rehearse what?"

"Act II. The part where the System stops pretending to be a game and starts being a test." The man reached into his coat and pulled out a photograph. He held it up. It showed Kael and Mira as children, maybe eight years old, standing in front of this same station, both of them grinning at the camera.

Kael's chest tightened. "Where did you get that?"

"It doesn't matter where I got it. It matters that I know it exists. And I know why the rift core reacted to your Panel. And I know why the script page wrote VERSION instead of a name." The man set the photograph on the floor, face up. "Sit down, Kael. This is the part where you learn what the Zero Slot really is."

Kael stayed standing for a long moment. Then, slowly, he sat down across from the man, in the seat where the light didn't touch.

The train began to move.

"Good," the man said. "Now let me tell you about the first version of the System. The one they buried before August City ever saw a Panel."

Kael's hands were cold. He forgot about the rift core. He forgot about the gate. He forgot about everything except the gray overcoat and the photograph and the sound of the train wheels ticking over the tracks like a countdown.

"I'm listening," he said.

The man smiled. It didn't reach his eyes. "The System wasn't built as a game. It was built as a cage. A very elegant, very patient cage. And the Zero Slot is the key that someone left in the lock."

Outside the train windows, the station blurred into darkness.

Kael's Panel flickered to life on its own, showing a single word in Slot Zero: VERSION.

"That's not a word," the man said, reading over his shoulder. "That's a fork in the road. One path leads to the next Gate. The other leads to the people who made the cage. You choose which one this train stops at."

Kael stared at the word. In the shadow of the car, with the train racing through the dark, it seemed less like a message and more like a drawn blade.

"The next Gate," he said. "I'm not ready for the makers yet."

The man's smile faded. "Most people aren't. That's why they never get off the train."

"You're not most people," Mira said.

Kael blinked. He was back on the platform. Standing in the gray morning light, still holding the script page. The rusted gate was closed behind him. The rift core was in Mira's outstretched hand.

"Kael?" Mira's voice was sharp. "You blacked out for a second. You grabbed the gate, and your hand went right through the sign like it wasn't there."

Kael looked down. His fingers were trembling. The script page had new words on it, darker than before: ACT II SCENE 1/2 — THE TRAIN WAS A QUESTION. THE ANSWER IS STILL IN TRANSIT.

"I saw him," Kael said slowly. "The man in the overcoat. He knew my name. He knew about the Zero Slot."

"Who?"

"He didn't tell me. But he said the System is a cage. And the Zero Slot is the key that someone left in the lock."

Mira went very quiet. Then she looked past him, at the sign on the rusted gate. The laminated word DO NOT ENTER had changed. Now it read: ACT II: FIND THE SECOND QUIET.

Kael read it aloud. The gate shivered. Somewhere deep in the station, a door slammed.

"Second quiet?" Mira said. "What's the first?"

"I don't know. Maybe the quiet after the Gate closed? Or the quiet inside my head when the train took off?" He pressed his palm against the sign. Cold. Ordinary. "But it's a clue. And we've got a train to catch—or not catch. The page said the answer is still in transit. So the next step isn't a door. It's a conversation."

"With who?"

"The man. He'll appear again. He said we'd rehearse Act II. That means we haven't reached the real scene yet." Kael turned away from the gate and looked at the rift core in Mira's hand. It was pulsing now, faster than before, as if waking up.

"Why did it react like that when you blacked out?" Mira asked.

"Because it's part of the same key," Kael said. The words came out before he knew he'd thought them. "The Slot Zero. The VERSION word. The rift core. The script page. They're all pieces of something the System didn't want to be found."

Mira tucked the core into her pocket. "Then we keep it. And we find the second quiet."

"Together."

They left the station through the north entrance, the ordinary sign reading AUGUST CENTRAL STATION — NORTH once again. The crew changed the departures board as if nothing had happened. The city kept its routines.

But Kael knew better. The performance wasn't over. It had barely begun.
