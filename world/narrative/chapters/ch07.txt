The lamp above the guild table buzzed.

Kael placed the Script Page in the center of the table. It had been a crumpled playbill since they defeated the Stagehand, its silver letters fading in and out like a weak radio signal. Beside it, the Rift Core pulsed with the same cold rhythm.

Mira leaned in, spear resting against her shoulder. “Still blank?”

“No,” Kael said. He didn’t need to open a quest window. He touched his Panel, and the impossible extra slot—Slot Zero—glowed at the bottom of his interface. He dragged the Script Page into it.

The page flattened until it was rigid. Ink bled upward and formed words:

GATE ONE
LOCATION: AUGUST CENTRAL STATION
TIME: 3:33 AM
THE OLD TRACKS REMEMBER

Mira read over his shoulder. “It’s like the Stagehand wrote his own sequel.”

From the doorway, the third member of Guild Alpha clapped his hands. He was a stocky man with a bandaged forearm, a healer who had almost died in the theater. “I hate sequels. They always kill someone.”

Kael pocketed the page. “Then we don’t let it.”

The healer crossed the room and looked at the page. “A Gate instead of a Rift. Version 1.0 changes the rules before we even learn the old ones.”

Mira picked up the Rift Core. It was warm, like a coal that had been cooling for an hour. “It reacts when the page is near. That’s how we know this isn’t a rumor. The System wrote it into the Stagehand’s script.”

Kael took the core from her and held it over the page. The core’s dark veins lit, and the Script Page’s last line shimmered:

GATE ONE — AUGUST CENTRAL STATION

“A Gate,” Kael said. “If the first Gate isn’t cleared, the station becomes a permanent instance.”

“Permanent instance?” Mira frowned. “Like a dungeon that doesn’t close?”

“Yes. And this city becomes the dungeon.”

The healer swore softly and pulled a wrist-mounted medkit from his pack. “Then we’d better get moving. Central Station is ten blocks away. We can be there before midnight and scout the old tracks.”

Kael glanced at his Panel. Level 4. His stat points were still zero, but his Agility had carried him through the theater. He had no points to spend, no new skills, only the weird Slot Zero that made no sense to anyone else.

He slotted the Script Page into his inventory and left the Rift Core in his pocket.

“Let’s go,” he said.

August Central Station had a vaulted glass roof that leaked the orange light of the city sky. The departure boards flickered, showing routes nobody recognized: NOTHING, MAIN, UNDERSIDE. A few night travelers sat on benches with paper cups, staring at nothing. Panels had arrived last year, but nobody had gotten used to them.

Mira stopped under the big clock. It read 9:14. “The old tracks are underneath. There used to be a platform for the night mail train.”

“I remember,” Kael said. “It’s been sealed since before the Panels came.”

They crossed the main hall. The healer kept his hand on his medkit. Kael led them past the ticket machines, down a corridor marked STAFF ONLY, and into a dusty stairwell that smelled of brake dust and wet newspaper.

At the bottom, a steel gate blocked the entrance to Platform 0. A padlock, thick as a fist, held the chain together.

The healer shook his head. “I can’t break that with my build.”

Mira stepped forward. Her spear was not just a weapon. She wedged the tip under the chain and pulled. The metal screamed, then popped. The chain fell in a heap.

“Good enough,” she said.

Platform 0 stretched into darkness. The old train tracks ran toward a brick wall where a tunnel had been walled up decades ago. Abandoned luggage lay scattered on the concrete. A station clock, cracked and dead, hung over the tunnel mouth. Its hands were frozen at 3:33.

Kael looked at the clock and then at the Script Page in his inventory. “The page didn’t say that.”

Mira walked to the clock and touched the glass. “But it was waiting for us.”

The Rift Core in Kael’s pocket grew cold. He took it out. The core was pulsing, but not in his hand—the pulse was coming from the wall.

“The gate is behind that wall,” Kael said. “Or inside it.”

The healer looked at the brick. “Should we break through now?”

Kael shook his head. “The time matters. The Stagehand’s script said 3:33. If we open it early, we might trigger something worse.”

Mira tapped the clock face. “Then we wait. We have six hours.”

They set up on the platform. Kael checked the brick wall for seams. The mortar was old, but between a few bricks he saw a faint blue light—a heartbeat behind stone.

At midnight, every Panel in the station chimed.

The voice that followed was flat, like poured concrete. It was the Warden, the anonymous System watcher who had never shown a face.

“Version 1.0 in effect at 3:33 AM. Rift structures are now Gate structures. First Gate confirmed: August Central Station, Platform 0. Clearing guild will receive a Guild Charter. Multiple parties may enter. Only one may claim the Gate Core.”

The sound cut off.

The healer exhaled. “A Guild Charter. That’s not just a title—that’s a real base, maybe a workshop, maybe storage.”

Mira looked at Kael. “Then we aren’t the only guild that will be here.”

“No,” Kael said. “But the Stagehand gave this to us.”

“Or lured us here.”

“Same thing.”

They waited. The clock above the tunnel ticked again, though the hands had not moved. Kael’s Panel occasionally pulsed. Slot Zero had gone quiet, still showing VERSION SHIFT: 1.0.

At 2:40, footsteps echoed down the stairwell. Kael turned, hand on his knife. A boy in a gray coat stood at the top of the platform steps, holding a Panel. Behind him, two others.

“This is Guild Alpha’s claim,” Mira called.

The boy held up his hands. “We’re not here to fight. Just to watch. The System said the first Gate is open to everyone.”

Kael shook his head. “Watch from farther away.”

They left. The healer let out a breath. “They’ll be back when the gate opens.”

“We’ll be ready.”

At 3:30, the air changed. The bricks began to sweat. Kael’s Panel slipped from his wrist and hovered in front of his face. The screen changed.

SYSTEM NOTICE
VERSION 1.0 LOADING
GATE DIRECTIONS LOCKED

His vision blurred. For a moment, he saw the wall not as brick but as a curtain. Behind it, a stage. A train station built from old sets, with ticket booths made of bones and a track that ran into a spotlight. The Stagehand’s hobby had not ended with the theater. It had only moved.

“Kael?” Mira’s voice cut through.

He blinked. The vision faded. The brick wall was cracking.

“3:33,” he said.

The cracked clock snapped its hands together. A sound like a stage bell rang through the platform. Then the brick wall split down the middle, not with the crash of stone but with the hiss of curtains being ripped open.

Blue light poured out. Not a rift—a Gate. It was a vertical seam of light and rust, shaped like a train door. The floor trembled. The old tracks hummed.

Every Panel in the station flashed.

VERSION 1.0 ACTIVE.

Kael’s Panel updated on its own. The version number changed from 0.9.2 to 1.0. A new subsystem appeared in the corner: GATES.

Then Slot Zero glowed. The words burned into place:

VERSION 1.0: GATE OPEN — CENTRAL STATION

The Gate’s light rippled. Something stepped through.

It was not a person. It was a long coat with a ticket machine for a head. Paper arms fluttered from its sleeves, each paper stamped with the word WAITING. It did not have eyes, but it turned toward them like a hound catching a scent.

Mira raised her spear. “It brought friends.”

Behind the ticket coat, more shapes moved in the blue light. The Gate was not a doorway to a dungeon. It was a doorway into a station that had been operating for years, waiting for passengers.

Kael’s First Strike skill flared along his Panel’s edge. He moved before the creature could take another step. His knife came up, caught the ticket machine’s face, and rang like a bell. The creature staggered but did not fall.

“Guild Alpha,” Kael said, holding the line, “we take the Gate together.”

Mira laughed. “I thought you hated sequels.”

The healer pulled a glowing bandage from his kit. “I do. So let’s give this one a bad review.”

The blue light grew. More ticket-coats pushed through. Behind them, a voice like tearing paper called out a train schedule.

Kael readied his blade. “After this, I’m buying better station clocks.”

Then the Gate opened wider, and the world around them turned into a platform no longer made of stone, but of a paper stage built by something that had been waiting for version 1.0 all along.
