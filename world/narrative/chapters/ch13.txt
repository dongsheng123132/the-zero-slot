The red spotlight pinned them to the stage. Kael’s eyes adjusted—the rigging above creaked, and a figure dropped from the shadows, landing silently on the boards. It was tall, wearing a long coat of crushed velvet, its face a featureless mask of polished wood. The Stage Manager.

Mira stepped back, her hand going to the knife at her belt. “Kael, that’s—the voice from the note.”

Kael held up the script page. The ink had rearranged itself: *Page twenty-six. Say it with a boom.* Below that, a single line: *THE QUIET WORD IS A DOOR; CLOSE IT WHEN THE STAGE MANAGER SPEAKS.*

His Slot Zero word pulsed in his vision—*VERSION 1.0: ACT II CUE — THE QUIET WORD IS A DOOR; CLOSE IT WHEN THE STAGE MANAGER SPEAKS.* The letters flickered, as if the System itself was struggling to keep the text stable.

“You have the token,” the Stage Manager said. Its voice came from everywhere, not just the mask. “The quiet sign. Use it. Open the door. Then close it while I am speaking.”

Kael’s jaw tightened. “Why would I do anything you say?”

The mask tilted. “Because if you don’t, the rift you closed will reopen. Not here—*inside* her.” It pointed a long finger at Mira. “She carried a fragment of the Stage’s silence. The quiet word can be a lock or a key. You have one use of it. Choose wisely.”

Mira’s face went pale. “He’s not lying. I felt something… heavy in my chest since we left the sewer rift. I thought it was just nerves.”

Kael swore under his breath. He pulled the quiet-sign-token from his inventory—a small, smooth disk of black stone with a single white line etched across its face. It hummed against his palm.

“How do I open the door?”

“Press the token to the stage floor and speak the word that is written on your script. The door will appear. I will speak. You must close it before I finish my sentence.” The Stage Manager folded its arms. “If you fail, the door stays open, and what comes through will feed on the silence she carries.”

Kael looked at Mira. She nodded, gripping her knife. “Do it.”

He knelt and pressed the token to the worn wooden stage. The script page glowed, and the word *SILENCE* burned in gold letters across the paper. He read it aloud.

“Silence.”

The stage split open with a sound like tearing velvet. A door of obsidian rose from the crack, twice as tall as a man, its surface covered in shifting glyphs. It swung open, revealing a corridor of absolute blackness. Cold air poured out, carrying whispers.

The Stage Manager began to speak. “The play must—”

Kael moved. He grabbed the door’s edge—it was ice-cold, burning his fingers—and started to pull it shut. But the whispers grew louder, and the door resisted, as if stuck on something.

“—continue,” the Stage Manager said, its voice slower now, drawn out.

Mira ran to help, throwing her weight against the door. “It’s too heavy! Kael, the token!”

Kael held the token in his other hand. The white line on the disk was bright. He understood—he had to close the door while the Stage Manager was still speaking, not after. The token was the key to lock it. He pressed the disk into the door’s surface. The black stone sank into the obsidian, and the door groaned shut.

The Stage Manager’s voice cut off mid-word. The door sealed with a click, then dissolved into motes of shadow that faded into the stage.

Silence.

The red spotlight dimmed to white. The Stage Manager was gone.

Kael let out a breath. “Did it work?”

Mira touched her chest. “The pressure… it’s gone.” She looked at Kael. “What was that thing?”

“The Stage Manager. It’s part of the System. Or something older.” Kael stood, his knees shaking. “The quiet word—it’s a door. And we just closed it. But I think we’re only in Act II now.”

He dismissed his Panel to check his stats. A notification popped up.

**System Notification:** *Act II, Scene 1 completed. +120 XP. The Stage Manager acknowledges your performance. The Quiet Word is now sealed.*

**New Knowledge:** *you understand that the Stage Manager is not an enemy—it is a gatekeeper. The Quiet Word’s door can only be closed while the gatekeeper speaks. You have earned its trust.*

Kael closed the window. “We need to get back to August City. The Guild Alpha people are probably wondering what happened at the theater.”

Mira sheathed her knife. “And after that?”

“I need to find out what the next Act is. And why my Panel has a Slot Zero.” He looked at the empty stage. “I think the System is a script we’re all following. And I’ve got a part that wasn’t in the original play.”

They walked out through the backstage exit, into the alley behind the theater. The sky above August City was gray, but the streetlights were flickering on as evening approached. A few players were gathered at the corner, wearing Guild Alpha tags, but they didn’t notice Kael and Mira.

Kael pulled out the script page. The ink was fading. On it, a new line had appeared: *ACT III: THE STAGE MANAGER’S INVITATION. Find the door that opens only when spoken to."*

He folded it and put it away. They had work to do.
